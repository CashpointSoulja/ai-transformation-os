# 5 Whys: why is AI adoption uneven across Product & Design?

I started from the symptom GitLab itself has written about: some teams found value fast and others never found an entry point. Then I asked "why" until I reached something a system could change.

**Symptom:** Weekly AI use ranges from about 25% in the PLG studio to 91% in Experience Research (seed data, modelled on that pattern).

1. **Why is use so uneven?**
   Teams that found a high-value workflow early, like research synthesis or release notes, use AI weekly. Teams that didn't are still experimenting one person at a time.

2. **Why haven't the lagging teams found a high-value workflow?**
   Their ideas never reach anyone who can prioritize and resource them. Requests are raised in Slack threads, 1:1s and hallway conversations, so the good ones aren't visible next to everything else.

3. **Why don't the ideas reach anyone?**
   There's no single intake route, and no shared way to judge one idea against another. Without a scoring model, the loudest or most senior request wins. Quieter teams stop raising them, and priorities churn as new voices arrive.

4. **Why is there no shared way to judge requests?**
   Nobody owned the function's AI portfolio end to end. Agents got built where enthusiasm happened to be, so some now have no owner, no eval and no KPI. With nobody accountable for the whole fleet, nobody could say what was working, what to reuse, or what to build next.

5. **Why did nobody own it?**
   AI capability arrived faster than the operating model. The tools were rolled out centrally, but the *what* and the *why* inside each function, meaning which problems to solve first and who maintains the result, had no owner. GitLab created the AI Transformation Owner role to fix exactly this.

**Root cause:** A missing operating system for the function's AI work. That means one intake route, an explainable prioritization model tied to business outcomes, named ownership of every agent, and a Champion network aimed at the lagging teams.

**What the product does about each "why":**

| Why | Countermeasure in the product |
|---|---|
| 1. Uneven high-value workflows | The demo wall and skills library spread proven workflows to every team: consume before build |
| 2. Ideas don't reach anyone | One intake portal in plain language, with Champions in the loop |
| 3. No shared way to judge | Triage agent: volume × frequency × pain, outcome weights, a visible trace, a two-sentence rationale |
| 4. Nobody owns the fleet | Fleet registry with unowned agents flagged, plus owner assignment, KPIs and eval status |
| 5. No owner for the *what*/*why* | The whole product is the ATO's operating surface. The coaching panel points at the lagging teams first. |
